GRIDLINE AI — DEMO SCRIPT

Presenter note:
Say “nearly 11 years of hourly data,” not 14 years.
The project contains 94,248 hourly weather records from January 2016 to October 2026.
These records were used for our research models.
The live 72-hour dashboard forecast uses future Open-Meteo weather with solar and wind engineering rules.


INTRODUCTION — BEYONDTECH

“Hello, everyone.
We are BeyondTech, and we are excited to present Gridline AI.
Our goal is to make renewable-energy operations easier to understand, plan, and manage.
Solar and wind power change with the weather, electricity prices change throughout the day, and operators must make quick decisions while respecting battery and grid limits.
Gridline AI brings these tasks into one clear platform.
It combines weather-based generation forecasting, market intelligence, mathematical optimization, controlled AI recommendations, human approval, and a complete audit trail.
For this hackathon, we use clearly labeled simulated farms, prices, and actions, while future weather can come from a live provider.
Let me begin with the 3D Farm Explorer.”


1. 3D FARM EXPLORER

The 3D Farm Explorer gives us a clear visual view of four demo assets: two solar farms and two wind farms.
I can move around the scene and select any farm to inspect it.

Each farm is connected to real features in the application.
It has its own capacity, battery, grid limits, availability, forecast, and operating settings.
The same farm identity is used by forecasting, optimization, decisions, and financial records.
This makes the 3D view a useful entry point, not just a visual effect.

For this hackathon, the farms and their current readings are simulated and clearly labeled.
The goal is to show how an operator could understand and manage several renewable assets from one place.
Next, I’ll open the Farm Detail page.”


2. FARM DETAIL

“The Farm Detail page gives us a closer look at one asset.
It shows current simulated generation, installed capacity, equipment availability, grid export, available grid space, battery charge, and reserve level.

The weather information matches the farm type.
Solar farms show sunlight, cloud cover, and temperature.
Wind farms show wind speed, direction, gusts, air density, and turbine availability.
These are the same kinds of inputs used for the future forecast.

The page also keeps different types of data separate.
Current readings are simulated operating data, while future values come from the weather forecast.
Financial value comes only from approved actions saved in the ledger.
We never show the full 72-hour estimate as money that has already been earned.
Next, I’ll show the Generation Outlook.”


3. GENERATION OUTLOOK

“This page shows expected generation for every hour over the next 72 hours.
It gets future weather from Open-Meteo and calculates how much power each farm can produce.

Solar calculations use the sun’s position, radiation, panel direction, temperature loss, inverter efficiency, availability, and capacity.
Solar is always zero at night.
Wind calculations adjust wind speed to turbine height and follow turbine cut-in, rated, and cut-out speeds.
Output can never exceed available farm capacity.

We also trained ExtraTrees research models after comparing three machine-learning approaches.
The data contains over 94,000 hourly weather records covering nearly 11 years.
Solar uses measured plant output where available.
Wind uses an engineering power curve because measured wind-farm output was unavailable.
The live dashboard uses the safer weather-and-engineering approach, and it does not display a made-up confidence percentage.”


4. PERFORMANCE

“The Performance page connects energy results with business value.
We can view today, seven days, thirty days, or the year to date.
It shows generated and expected energy, capacity use, availability, solar and wind contribution, battery discharge, and curtailed energy.

The important idea here is honest reporting.
If we do not have the final outcome needed to calculate forecast accuracy, the page says it is unavailable.
It does not invent a percentage.
Lost revenue is also unavailable unless we have enough price and curtailment data to calculate it properly.

Financial value comes from the saved simulation ledger.
A value is recorded only after a user approves the first hour of a recommendation.
The remaining 71 hours stay as a forecast.
This makes it easy to tell the difference between current, forecast, approved, and unavailable information.”


5. OPERATIONS

“Operations is where a person reviews settings and recommendations.
We can change battery reserve, grid export limits, battery mode, operating mode, and farm output limits.
The optimizer uses these updated settings in its next run.

The Portfolio Decision card shows expected generation, export, storage, purchases, curtailment, battery charge, and projected value.
It also explains why the plan was selected and its main trade-off.

The confidence score is based on real checks: an optimal solver result, independent safety validation, complete 72-hour inputs, and three compared strategies.
The current score can reach 90%.
The missing 10% represents forecast uncertainty that is not yet calibrated, so this is a decision-readiness score—not a promise of profit.

The plan covers 72 hours, but approval applies only the first hour across all farms.
Repeated, expired, stale, or conflicting approvals are rejected, and no command reaches real equipment.”


6. AGENT COMMAND CENTER

“The Agent Command Center shows how the system works together.
Generation Forecasting creates the 72-hour energy outlook.
Numerical Market Forecasting creates 288 fifteen-minute demo prices and combines them into 72 hourly prices.

Market Intelligence collects articles from websites we configure.
For this demo, those websites contain clearly marked simulated articles.
An AI model finds possible market drivers, risks, and opportunities, but it cannot invent prices or control a farm.

The optimizer uses math to compare selling, storing, buying, and limiting energy while following battery and grid rules.
The Decision Agent can ask it to compare up to three strategies.
It must choose a checked plan and cannot approve it.

This page shows ready or completed components, the decision confidence, market path, first-hour actions, workflow history, and ledger records.
This makes the AI process easy to explain and review.”


7. ALERTS AND CLOSING

“The Alerts page gives the operator a simple list of items that may need attention.
Alerts can cover grid space, equipment availability, battery reserve, weather, forecasts, or an AI workflow.
Each alert shows what happened, the affected farm, the time, and the seriousness.
An operator can acknowledge it without removing its history.

Gridline AI brings the full process into one application.
We view the farms, estimate renewable generation from future weather, create a clear demo market path, 
find a valid fleet plan, compare checked options with a limited Decision Agent, and require human approval.
The system then saves the state change and financial result for later review.

The farms, prices, business settings, and actions are simulated for this demo, while weather can come from a live provider.
The same design can later connect to approved real data without removing the safety, approval, and audit steps.”
